The Oral Microbiome, Explained in Plain English

Your mouth hosts hundreds of bacterial species. Here is what that community does and why balance matters.

By the DentaBiome Info editorial team · Updated November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

In this article
  1. A crowded neighborhood
  2. When balance tips
  3. What helps keep it steady
  4. Where probiotics and postbiotics come in

A crowded neighborhood

Your mouth is one of the busiest microbial habitats in the body. Hundreds of bacterial species live on your teeth, gums, tongue and cheeks, along with fungi and other microbes. Together they make up the oral microbiome.

Most of these organisms are harmless, and many are helpful. They take up space and nutrients that troublemakers would otherwise use, and they're part of how your mouth stays in a steady state.

When balance tips

Trouble tends to start when the community shifts. As the National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research explains, bacteria that feed on sugars produce acids that can wear down enamel over time. Plaque that isn't removed can irritate the gums. Frequent sugar, skipped brushing and a dry mouth all push the balance in the wrong direction.

Think of it like a garden. Brushing and flossing are the weeding, saliva is the irrigation and sugar is the fertilizer the weeds like best.

What helps keep it steady

  • Brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste
  • Cleaning between teeth daily
  • Cutting back on frequent sugary snacks and sipping
  • Drinking water and keeping saliva flowing
  • Regular dental cleanings

Where probiotics and postbiotics come in

Probiotics are live microorganisms. Postbiotics are generally described as non-living compounds derived from them. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements notes that research on probiotics is promising for some uses and inconclusive for others, and that effects depend on the specific strain.
